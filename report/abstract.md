The Census Bureau fields 47 demographic survey instruments containing approximately 7,000 questions, each designed for a specific analytical purpose. No comprehensive map has existed showing where these surveys overlap, where their questions are compatible, and where the differences matter. Building that map manually requires a research team to compare thousands of questions across dozens of instruments, a task that takes months and exceeds practical limits not because of complexity, but because of scale. No individual, however expert, holds 7,000 questions in working memory at once.

This research uses AI-assisted methods to build that map at scale: multiple models from competing vendors independently rate question pairs, with structured arbitration for disagreements. Across two major survey pairs (CPS and FoodAPS, each scored against ACS as an anchor survey), approximately 45% of questions have at least one viable harmonization path. Roughly 16% are directly interchangeable through simple recoding, roughly 29% are linkable with documented statistical adjustments, and roughly 55% measure fundamentally different constructs. Three independent AI models from competing vendors reach near-perfect agreement on harmonization judgments, confirming the task is well-defined and results are not driven by any single model's bias. Harmonization rates are stable across survey pairs, suggesting a general property of the data rather than a survey-specific artifact. The complete analysis was conducted in weeks at a total API cost under $100. The full pipeline, data, and code are public and reproducible.
