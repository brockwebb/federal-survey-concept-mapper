# Report 03 Framing: AI-Assisted Harmonization Analysis

## The Feasibility Transformation

Traditional survey harmonization literature assumes manual expert review as the primary methodology. The National Academies (2018) recommends 5-10 year implementation horizons with $3-5M near-term investment for establishing content harmonization teams, conducting multi-year pilot studies, and iterating through sequential concept evaluation.

**This timeline assumes constraints that AI-assisted analysis removes.**

### Traditional Approach
- Cross-agency harmonization teams (8-12 members per National Academies)
- Manual review of question pairs for concept alignment
- Sequential pilot studies with multi-year data collection
- Iterative refinement requiring institutional coordination across budget cycles

### AI-Assisted Approach (This Project)
- Single researcher + LLM tools
- Batch processing of thousands of question pairs in hours
- Rapid iteration on taxonomy without institutional overhead
- Total cost: <$100 in API fees

### What Changes

| Dimension | Traditional | AI-Assisted |
|-----------|-------------|-------------|
| Timeline | 5-10 years | Days to weeks |
| Cost | $3-5M near-term | <$100 |
| Personnel | Cross-agency teams | Single analyst |
| Iteration speed | Budget-cycle dependent | Same-day |
| Scope feasibility | Limited by labor | Limited by imagination |

### What Doesn't Change

AI-assisted analysis does **not** replace:
- Subject matter expertise for validating taxonomy
- Institutional authority for implementing changes
- Stakeholder negotiation for breaking time series
- Legal/privacy frameworks for data sharing
- Operational capacity for fielding revised instruments

### The Value Proposition

The goal is **not** staff reduction. Statistics Netherlands' "40% staffing reduction" framing reflects budget-office priorities, not methodological values.

Better framing: **AI assistance redirects expert time from tractable-but-tedious crosswalk work to high-value survey design innovation.**

What was previously resource-prohibitive becomes routine:
- Comprehensive pairwise analysis across entire survey ecosystems
- Rapid prototyping of alternative taxonomies
- Quantitative prevalence estimates for constraint types
- Decision frameworks grounded in systematic evidence rather than selective examples

### Implications for Federal Statistical System

1. **Democratized capability**: Any agency can now conduct harmonization analysis that previously required specialized teams
2. **Evidence-based prioritization**: Systematic constraint prevalence data enables rational resource allocation
3. **Accelerated iteration**: Taxonomy refinement cycles compress from years to days
4. **Expanded scope**: Analysis of 7,000 questions across 48 surveys becomes feasible for proof-of-concept

This project demonstrates the feasibility transformation. The harmonization constraints taxonomy (Report 03) would traditionally require months of expert review. AI-assisted analysis makes it a tractable research contribution.
