## Theoretical Basis

This taxonomy synthesizes multiple established frameworks from the survey methodology literature:

### Primary Sources

**1. Wolf, C., Schneider, S. L., Behr, D., & Joye, D. (2016).** Harmonizing survey questions between cultures and over time. In C. Wolf, D. Joye, T. W. Smith, & Y. Fu (Eds.), *The SAGE Handbook of Survey Methodology* (pp. 502-524). SAGE Publications. https://doi.org/10.4135/9781473957893

*Contribution:* Establishes core barrier categories for question-level harmonization including reference period differences, concept/construct differences, question stem differences, response format differences, and questionnaire context differences. Direct mapping to our Level 1 taxonomy.

**2. Slomczynski, K. M., & Tomescu-Dubrow, I. (2018).** Basic principles of survey data recycling. In T. P. Johnson, B.-E. Pennell, I. A. L. Stoop, & B. Dorer (Eds.), *Advances in Comparative Survey Methods: Multinational, Multiregional, and Multicultural Contexts (3MC)* (pp. 937-962). Wiley.

*Contribution:* Ex-post harmonization paradigm, methodological variability taxonomy between survey projects, quality control frameworks for harmonization procedures.

**3. Fortier, I., Doiron, D., Little, J., Ferretti, V., L'Heureux, F., Stolk, R. P., Knoppers, B. M., Hudson, T. J., & Burton, P. R. (2011).** Is rigorous retrospective harmonization possible? Application of the DataSHaPER approach across 53 large studies. *International Journal of Epidemiology, 40*(5), 1314-1328. https://doi.org/10.1093/ije/dyr106

*Contribution:* DataSHaPER compatibility classification framework, empirical finding that 38% of assessment items could be harmonized across 53 studies (6.9 million participants). Identifies variable characteristics (reference period, individual targeted) and study characteristics (data collection mode) associated with harmonization potential.

**4. Fortier, I., Raina, P., van den Heuvel, E. R., Griffith, L. E., Craig, C., Saliba, M., Doiron, D., Stolk, R. P., Knoppers, B. M., Ferretti, V., Granda, P., & Burton, P. (2017).** Maelstrom Research guidelines for rigorous retrospective data harmonization. *International Journal of Epidemiology, 46*(1), 103-115. https://doi.org/10.1093/ije/dyw075

*Contribution:* Formalized guidelines for retrospective harmonization based on workshops with 34 major international research initiatives. Establishes systematic procedures for compatibility assessment.

**5. Saris, W. E., & Gallhofer, I. N. (2014).** *Design, Evaluation, and Analysis of Questionnaires for Survey Research* (2nd ed.). Wiley.

*Contribution:* Response scale quality dimensions including number of categories, scale polarity, labeling conventions, and midpoint presence. Foundation for RS (Response Scale) constraint subtypes.

**6. Tomescu-Dubrow, I., Wolf, C., Slomczynski, K. M., & Jenkins, J. C. (Eds.). (2024).** *Survey Data Harmonization in the Social Sciences*. Wiley. ISBN: 978-1119712176

*Contribution:* Comprehensive state-of-the-art reference covering ex-ante and ex-post harmonization methodologies. Includes updated Maelstrom Research chapter by Fortier and SDR framework applications.

## Taxonomy Structure

### Level 1: Constraint Type (7 categories)

| Code | Constraint Type | Definition | Primary Literature Source |
|------|-----------------|------------|---------------------------|
| TC | **Temporal** | Reference period or timing differences | Wolf et al. (2016); Fortier et al. (2011) |
| CC | **Construct** | Concept definition or operationalization differences | Wolf et al. (2016); Fortier et al. (2011) |
| PC | **Population/Coverage** | Universe, frame, or sample design differences | Slomczynski & Tomescu-Dubrow (2018) |
| RS | **Response Scale** | Scale type, categories, or format differences | Saris & Gallhofer (2014) |
| MC | **Mode/Context** | Interview mode or questionnaire context differences | Wolf et al. (2016) |
| PM | **Processing/Metadata** | Coding, weighting, or documentation differences | Oleksiyenko et al. (2018) |
| NHB | **No Harmonization Barrier** | Questions are functionally equivalent or near-duplicates | Added v1.1 (2026-01-29) |

---

### Level 2: Subtypes

#### TC - Temporal Constraints
*Based on Wolf et al. (2016) "reference period differences" and Fortier et al. (2011) finding that reference period is associated with harmonization potential*

| Code | Subtype | Definition | Example |
|------|---------|------------|---------|
| TC.1 | Reference period length | Different recall windows | 7-day vs 12-month |
| TC.2 | Temporal framing | Point-in-time vs habitual vs retrospective | "last week" vs "usually" vs "ever" |
| TC.3 | Calendar alignment | Fixed vs rolling reference periods | Calendar year vs "past 12 months" |

#### CC - Construct Constraints
*Based on Wolf et al. (2016) "concept differences" and Fortier et al. (2011) "inferential equivalence" requirement*

| Code | Subtype | Definition | Example |
|------|---------|------------|---------|
| CC.1 | Concept definition | Different meaning of core term | "Employment" including/excluding unpaid family work |
| CC.2 | Operationalization | Different behavioral indicators | "Food insecurity" via different question batteries |
| CC.3 | Boundary conditions | Different thresholds or cutoffs | Income brackets with different boundaries |
| CC.4 | Scope inclusions | Different components counted | "Income" including/excluding specific sources |

#### PC - Population/Coverage Constraints
*Based on Slomczynski & Tomescu-Dubrow (2018) "methodological variability between projects" including sampling design*

| Code | Subtype | Definition | Example |
|------|---------|------------|---------|
| PC.1 | Universe definition | Target population differs | Civilian non-institutional vs all residents |
| PC.2 | Frame exclusions | Different exclusions from sampling | Group quarters, territories, institutionalized |
| PC.3 | Age bounds | Different age eligibility | 15+ vs 16+ vs 18+ |
| PC.4 | Geographic scope | Different geographic coverage | 50 states vs 50 states + territories |

#### RS - Response Scale Constraints
*Based on Saris & Gallhofer (2014) scale characteristic dimensions*

| Code | Subtype | Definition | Example |
|------|---------|------------|---------|
| RS.1 | Scale type | Fundamentally different response formats | Binary vs 5-point ordinal vs continuous |
| RS.2 | Category structure | Different number/boundaries of categories | 5-point vs 7-point Likert |
| RS.3 | Anchoring/labels | Different verbal anchors or direction | "Strongly agree" anchoring differences |
| RS.4 | Numeric vs verbal | Numeric scale vs labeled categories | 0-10 slider vs verbal frequency |

#### MC - Mode/Context Constraints
*Based on Wolf et al. (2016) "questionnaire context differences"*

| Code | Subtype | Definition | Example |
|------|---------|------------|---------|
| MC.1 | Interview mode | Different data collection modes | CATI vs web self-administered |
| MC.2 | Question routing | Different skip patterns or filters | Question asked of subset vs all |
| MC.3 | Contextual priming | Preceding questions affect interpretation | Employment section before vs after income |
| MC.4 | Proxy response | Proxy vs self-report rules | Household reference person vs each individual |

#### PM - Processing/Metadata Constraints
*Based on Oleksiyenko et al. (2018) "processing errors in cross-national surveys"*

| Code | Subtype | Definition | Example |
|------|---------|------------|---------|
| PM.1 | Coding schemes | Different classification or coding | Different occupation coding systems |
| PM.2 | Derived variables | Different algorithms for constructed variables | Different poverty calculation methods |
| PM.3 | Documentation gaps | Insufficient metadata to assess comparability | Missing questionnaire context |

#### NHB - No Harmonization Barrier
*Added v1.1 (2026-01-29) based on empirical finding during three-rater coding*

| Code | Subtype | Definition | Example |
|------|---------|------------|---------|
| NHB.0 | No barrier | Questions are functionally equivalent or near-duplicates requiring only minor standardization | Identical sex/gender questions across surveys |

*Note: This code emerged from 11 pairs where all three raters returned null/None for primary_barrier with F1 feasibility. These represent pairs that slipped through Report 02's consolidation filter or where raters disagreed with the prior non-consolidatable classification.*

---

### Level 3: Specific Conflict (Free Text)

Captures the exact nature of the difference for each coded pair.

Examples:
- "30-day vs 12-month reference period"
- "Employment includes unpaid family workers in Survey A but not Survey B"
- "5-point Likert vs 4-point Likert with no neutral option"

---

## Feasibility Classification

Adapted from Fortier et al. (2011, 2017) DataSHaPER/Maelstrom compatibility framework:

| Code | Feasibility | Definition | Action Required | Literature Equivalent |
|------|-------------|------------|-----------------|----------------------|
| F1 | **Direct recode** | Mechanically transformable | Simple recoding/collapsing | "Fully compatible" |
| F2 | **Statistical adjustment** | Requires modeling or assumptions | Bridging studies, imputation | "Partially compatible" |
| F3 | **Incompatible** | Fundamentally different constructs | Not harmonizable without re-fielding | "Non-equivalent" |

*Note: Fortier et al. (2011) found that across 53 studies, 38% of variable-study pairs could be harmonized, with "essential" variables achieving 62% harmonization potential.*

## Coding Schema (Per Question Pair)

```
pair_id: str                    # Unique identifier
survey_a: str                   # Source survey
survey_b: str                   # Target survey (typically ACS)
question_a: str                 # Question text from survey A
question_b: str                 # Question text from survey B
concept_match: bool             # Do they measure same concept? (from Report 02)
consolidation_feasible: bool    # Can they be consolidated? (from Report 02)

# If not consolidation_feasible:
constraint_type: str            # Level 1 code (TC, CC, PC, RS, MC, PM, NHB)
constraint_subtype: str         # Level 2 code (e.g., TC.1)
specific_conflict: str          # Level 3 free text
feasibility: str                # F1, F2, or F3
reasoning: str                  # Coder's explanation

# Multiple constraints possible:
additional_constraints: list    # If >1 constraint applies
```

---

## Coding Procedure

1. **Sample selection**: Start with "not consolidatable" pairs from Report 02 merged CSVs
2. **Initial coding**: Code first 30 pairs, refine taxonomy as needed (saturation)
3. **Stabilization**: When no new categories emerge for 10 consecutive pairs, lock taxonomy
4. **Full coding**: Apply finalized schema to all non-consolidatable pairs
5. **Reliability check**: Dual-code 10% sample, compute Cohen's kappa
